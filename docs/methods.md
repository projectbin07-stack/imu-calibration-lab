# Methods

The equations behind each calculation, the choices made where the
literature leaves room, and where they come from. Everything is implemented
from these equations in `src/core/`; no code was copied from other
libraries.

Notation: vectors are 3x1 columns; `|v|` is the Euclidean norm. Internally
accelerations are in g, angular rates in deg/s, magnetic field in the log's
unit (microtesla by default) and temperature in degC.

All results bundled with this repository (tests, examples, README output,
screenshots) come from **synthetic data** generated by `src/core/synth.js`
with known parameters. None of the methods has been validated on a physical
sensor in this project.

## 1. Static-segment detection

Calibration logs alternate between holding the sensor still and moving it.
The detector computes, for every sample, the variance of each axis in a
centred moving window of `w` seconds (prefix sums, O(N)) and sums the three
axes:

    V_a(k) = var_x(k) + var_y(k) + var_z(k)

A sample is static when `V_a(k) <= T_a` (and, when a gyroscope is present,
the same test passes on the gyroscope variance with its own threshold).
Runs shorter than `minSeconds` are discarded.

The variance threshold follows the idea of Tedaldi et al. (2014), who set
it as a multiple of the variance measured during an initial rest period.
Here no dedicated rest period is required: the threshold is
`T = k * Q20(V)`, `k` times the 20th percentile of the moving variance over
the whole log. In a calibration log the sensor is still for most of the
time, so the 20th percentile is a noise-only window; motion by hand raises
the variance by orders of magnitude. Defaults: `w = 0.5 s`, `k = 10`,
`minSeconds = 1.5`. A floor of (1e-4 g)^2 prevents a zero threshold on
quantised, noise-free data.

Limits: a slow, constant-rate rotation about the gravity vector keeps the
accelerometer variance low (gravity does not change in the body frame); the
gyroscope variance test catches it only if the rate is not constant. If
less than about 20 % of the log is static, the percentile is no longer a
noise estimate: set a fixed threshold (`--threshold`) instead.

Adjacent segments whose mean directions differ by less than 3 deg are merged
into one pose (a hold interrupted by a bump).

## 2. Accelerometer calibration

Model, as in Tedaldi et al. (2014) and Skog and Handel (2006):

    a_cal = M (a_raw - b)

`b` is the bias and `M` holds the scale factors and the axis misalignment.
At rest the true specific force has magnitude 1 g, pointing up, so the axis
that reads about +1 g is the one pointing up.

### 2.1 Six-position (bias and scale, 6 parameters)

The classic tumble test (IEEE Std 1293-1998; Titterton and Weston 2004): for each axis i, with `u_i` and `d_i` the mean readings with
that axis up and down,

    b_i = (u_i + d_i) / 2,    s_i = (u_i - d_i) / 2,    M = diag(1 / s_i)

It ignores misalignment and assumes each pose is aligned with gravity. A
pose counts as axis-aligned when gravity is within 10 deg of a sensor axis;
several poses on the same axis are averaged.

### 2.2 Twelve-parameter linear least squares

With a known reference vector `r_k` (+/-1 g on one axis) for each
axis-aligned pose, `r_k = M a_k + c` is linear in the 12 unknowns of
`[M | c]`, solved row by row by least squares; then `b = -M^-1 c`. This is
the six-position least-squares calibration of STMicroelectronics AN3192
(2010). It needs at least 4 poses that span three dimensions; 6 is the
usual set. The body frame is the frame the poses were aligned to, so the
result is only as good as the alignment: the tests show that a 2 deg error
in placing each pose moves elements of `M` by more than 5e-3, while the
nine-parameter fit is unaffected.

### 2.3 Nine-parameter fit on |a| = 1 g

With poses in any orientations, minimise over the poses' mean readings

    sum_k ( |M (a_k - b)| - 1 )^2

(Skog and Handel 2006; Fong, Ong and Nee 2008; Tedaldi et al. 2014). The
magnitude is invariant to a rotation of the body frame, so only 9 of the 12
parameters are observable. `M` is taken lower triangular: the calibrated x
axis is the sensor x axis and the calibrated y axis lies in the sensor x-y
plane (the same convention as Tedaldi et al.'s triangular misalignment
matrix). Solved by Levenberg-Marquardt (Levenberg 1944; Marquardt 1963) with
the analytic Jacobian

    d r_k / d M_ij = v_i u_j / |v|,    d r_k / d b = -(M^T v) / |v|,
    u = a_k - b,    v = M u

starting from `b = 0`, `M = I / mean |a_k|`. At least 9 poses are required;
more poses in well-spread orientations make the fit better determined. The condition number of
`J^T J` at the solution is reported and a warning is given above 1e8.

`auto` picks the nine-parameter fit with 9 or more poses, otherwise the
twelve-parameter fit when all six axis directions are present.

### 2.4 Reported quantities

- Scale factor per axis and non-orthogonality: with `D = M^-1`, row i of
  `D` is the sensitive direction of raw axis i in the calibrated frame. Its
  length is the scale factor (raw g per true g) and `90 deg - angle(D_i,
D_j)` the non-orthogonality of axes i and j.
- Residuals: `|a| - 1 g` for each pose mean, before (raw) and after
  calibration, RMS and maximum; the same per static sample for the
  histograms (there, sensor noise dominates after calibration).

Limits: gravity is assumed to be exactly 1 g (local gravity varies by about
0.5 % over the Earth's surface; `m/s^2` input is divided by standard gravity
9.80665); linear model only (no cross-axis nonlinearity or g^2 terms); no
temperature dependence of the accelerometer.

## 3. Gyroscope calibration

Model:

    w_cal = (w_raw - b(T)) / s

- **Bias** `b`: mean rate over the static segments (detected with the
  accelerometer and gyroscope variance). Noise standard deviation is the
  spread of the static samples about the bias model.
- **Temperature model** (when a temperature column exists): the static
  samples are binned by temperature (default 1 degC), bins with fewer than
  50 samples are dropped, and a straight line `b(T) = b0 + k (T - T_ref)` is
  fitted per axis by least squares weighted by the bin counts, with `T_ref`
  the weighted mean temperature. A linear model over the logged range only;
  extrapolation outside it is not checked.
- **Scale factor** `s` from a rotation through a known angle `theta` (a
  rate-table or hand turn of 360 deg, returning to the starting pose):

        s_i = (integral over the turn of (w_i - b_i) dt) / theta

  integrated with the trapezoid rule. Each moving interval between two
  static segments is a candidate; for axis i the interval used is the one
  with the largest angle on that axis that is at least 80 % of the total
  rotation (a single-axis turn) and at least 75 % of `theta`. The other two
  integrated angles are reported; for a turn about one sensor axis they
  indicate cross-coupling or how well the turn was aligned.

This is the integrated-rate form of the rate-transfer test of IEEE Std
952-1997, with the known angle replacing a
calibrated rate table. A hand rotation that ends 3 deg away from the
starting orientation gives a 0.8 % scale error; use a fixture (a box edge
against a wall, a turntable) for better than that. Cross-axis sensitivity
and g-sensitivity are not estimated.

## 4. Magnetometer: hard and soft iron by ellipsoid fitting

Model (Ozyagcilar 2015, Freescale AN4246; Vasconcelos et al. 2011):

    m_raw = S m_body + c

with `c` the hard-iron offset and `S` the soft-iron (and scale/misalignment)
matrix. With `|m_body| = R` constant, the raw samples lie on the ellipsoid
`(m - c)^T A (m - c) = 1`, `A = (S S^T)^-1 / R^2`. The correction is

    m_cal = W (m_raw - c),    W = R_hat sqrtm(A)

`sqrtm` is the symmetric square root, so `W` is symmetric: the rotation part
of `S` is not observable from the field magnitude alone (Vasconcelos et al. 2011) and is left out. `R_hat` is the geometric mean of the fitted
semi-axes, so the corrected field keeps the volume-equivalent radius in the
log's units.

### 4.1 Li-Griffiths ellipsoid-specific least squares

Li and Griffiths (2004) fit the general quadric

    a x^2 + b y^2 + c z^2 + 2f yz + 2g xz + 2h xy + 2p x + 2q y + 2r z + d = 0

minimising the algebraic distance `|D v|^2` (D the n x 10 design matrix)
under the constraint `4J - I^2 = 1`, `I = a + b + c`,
`J = ab + bc + ca - f^2 - g^2 - h^2`, which guarantees an ellipsoid whose
shortest axis is at least half the longest. With `S = D^T D` partitioned
into S11 (6x6), S12 (6x4), S22 (4x4), the solution satisfies

    (S11 - S12 S22^-1 S21) v1 = lambda C1 v1,    v2 = -S22^-1 S21 v1

where C1 holds the constraint (`-1` on the diagonal and `1` off it in the
top-left 3x3 block, `-4 I` in the bottom-right block). The paper shows that
exactly one eigenvalue is positive; its eigenvector is the fit. This
implementation solves the generalised problem as a symmetric one through
the Cholesky factor `L` of the reduced scatter matrix
(`L^-1 C1 L^-T w = (1/lambda) w`, `v1 = L^-T w`) with Jacobi rotations; a
ridge of 1e-12 times the trace keeps `L` defined for exact data. Points are
centred and scaled to unit RMS radius before the fit and the result mapped
back. From the quadric: `c = -A_q^-1 [p q r]`, `A = A_q / (c^T A_q c - d)`.

For axis ratios below 0.5 the constraint may not give the least-squares
optimum (the paper proposes a search for that case, not implemented); a
warning is shown.

### 4.2 Reported quantities

- **Sphericity**: smallest / largest semi-axis of the fitted ellipsoid,
  before (raw data) and after (a second fit to the corrected data).
- **Magnitude spread**: standard deviation / mean of `|m|`, raw (about the
  origin) and corrected.
- **Coverage**: 3 x the smallest eigenvalue of the covariance of the
  corrected unit directions (1 for a uniform sphere, near 0 when the sensor
  was only turned in one plane). Below 0.3 a warning asks for more
  orientations.
- **Heading error estimate** for a level compass, heading
  `psi = atan2(-y, x)`, with field strength `R_hat` and dip angle `I`
  (from the angle between the accelerometer and the corrected field,
  `angle = 90 deg + I`, median over the log; or entered; or 45 deg):
  - uncorrected: the fitted distortion `S = W^-1` and offset `c` are applied
    to a horizontal field rotated through 360 deg and the largest heading
    error is reported. A pure offset `e` across a horizontal field `Bh`
    gives `asin(e / Bh)`, and a 2:1 axis ratio gives 19.47 deg (both checked
    in the tests).
  - corrected: `atan(RMS radial residual / Bh)`, treating the residual
    tangential error as being as large as the radial one. An estimate, not a
    measurement: tilt-compensation error and local field disturbances are
    not included.

## 5. Allan variance

Definitions from IEEE Std 952-1997 (Annex C) and IEEE Std 647-2006, with the
overlapping estimator (Riley 2008, NIST SP 1065; El-Sheimy,
Hou and Niu 2008). For rate samples `x_k` at period `tau0`, integrate
`theta_k = tau0 * sum_{i<k} x_i` (k = 0..N) and for cluster size `m`,
`tau = m tau0`:

    AVAR(tau) = 1 / (2 tau^2 (N - 2m)) * sum_{k=0}^{N-2m} (theta_{k+2m} - 2 theta_{k+m} + theta_k)^2

Cluster sizes are log-spaced (10 per decade by default) from 1 to
`floor((N - 1) / 2)`; the mean is removed before integrating (it does not
change AVAR and keeps the sums small).

### 5.1 Read-off of the noise terms

On a log-log plot of `sigma(tau) = sqrt(AVAR)` each noise process has a
characteristic slope (IEEE Std 952-1997, Annex C):

| Term                                | Slope | `sigma^2(tau)`      | Read-off                  |
| ----------------------------------- | ----- | ------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Angle (velocity) random walk `N`    | -1/2  | `N^2 / tau`         | line value at `tau = 1 s` |
| Bias instability `B`                | 0     | `(2 ln 2 / pi) B^2` | `B = sigma_min / 0.664`   |
| Rate (acceleration) random walk `K` | +1/2  | `K^2 tau / 3`       | line value at `tau = 3 s` |

Implementation:

- Local slope at each point by central differences in log-log.
- `N`: the longest run of points whose local slope is within 0.08 of -1/2
  (at least 3 points); `N` is the geometric mean of `sigma sqrt(tau)` over
  the run.
- `B`: the minimum of the curve among points with at least about six
  independent clusters (single-point error <= 30 %); one random-walk
  realisation can dip far below the trend at the longest cluster times. If
  the minimum is the last such point the curve was still falling and the
  value is reported as an upper bound (`<=`): the log was too short.
- `K`: the longest run after the minimum with local slope within 0.2 of
  +1/2 (at least 2 points; the long-tau points scatter more), coefficient
  `sigma sqrt(3 / tau)` averaged in log.
- Points with fewer than about three independent clusters (single-point
  error > 50 %) are plotted but not used for `N` or `K`.

The minimum also contains some white and random-walk noise, so `B` reads
high when the flat region is narrow (the tests show 0-15 % high on an
analytic curve with a wide floor). Quantisation noise (slope -1), flicker
modelled as Gauss-Markov bumps, and rate ramp (+1) are not separated.

Unit conversions for the gyroscope: `N[deg/sqrt(h)] = 60 N[deg/s *
sqrt(s)]` (deg/s * sqrt(s) is the same as deg/s/sqrt(Hz), the noise
density); `B[deg/h] = 3600 B[deg/s]`; `K[deg/h/sqrt(h)] = 3600^1.5 K[deg/s/sqrt(s)]`. Accelerometer
values are given in micro-g: `N` in ug/sqrt(Hz), `B` in ug, `K` in
ug/sqrt(s).

### 5.2 Confidence

The fractional 1-sigma error of a single Allan deviation point with `N/m`
clusters is approximately

    delta = 1 / sqrt(2 (N/m - 1))

(El-Sheimy, Hou and Niu 2008, after Papoulis). It is drawn as the
error bar of each point and quoted next to each read-off: for `N` and `K`
the error of the longest-tau point used, for `B` the error at the minimum.
This formula treats the clusters as independent; the overlapping estimator
is somewhat better than that and the equivalent-degrees-of-freedom method of
Riley (2008) would give tighter, noise-type-specific chi-square
intervals. It is used here because it is simple and conservative.

### 5.3 Logging length

To see bias instability at `tau_B` you need a log several times longer: at
`T = 9 tau_B` there are only 9 independent clusters (delta = 25 %). A MEMS
gyroscope's minimum is typically at tens to hundreds of seconds, so log at
least 1 hour; rate random walk needs several hours. El-Sheimy et al. (2008)
also note that the sample rate should be several times the sensor
bandwidth: set the MPU6050 digital low-pass filter (DLPF_CFG) and log at a
rate well above its cut-off.

## 6. MPU6050 register scaling

From the InvenSense MPU-6000/MPU-6050 Register Map and Descriptions,
RM-MPU-6000A-00 rev 4.2 (2013):

| AFS_SEL | Accelerometer range | LSB/g | FS_SEL | Gyroscope range | LSB/(deg/s) |
| ------- | ------------------- | ----- | ------ | --------------- | ----------- |
| 0       | +/-2 g              | 16384 | 0      | +/-250 deg/s    | 131         |
| 1       | +/-4 g              | 8192  | 1      | +/-500 deg/s    | 65.5        |
| 2       | +/-8 g              | 4096  | 2      | +/-1000 deg/s   | 32.8        |
| 3       | +/-16 g             | 2048  | 3      | +/-2000 deg/s   | 16.4        |

(section 4.17, registers 59-64, ACCEL_XOUT..ZOUT; section 4.19, registers
67-72, GYRO_XOUT..ZOUT; ranges set in ACCEL_CONFIG 0x1C and GYRO_CONFIG
0x1B). Die temperature (section 4.18): `T [degC] = TEMP_OUT / 340 + 36.53`.
The gyroscope sensitivities are rounded in the datasheet (131 x 250 =
32750, not 32768).

The synthetic noise defaults use the typical figures of the MPU-6000/6050
Product Specification PS-MPU-6000A-00 rev 3.4 (2013): gyroscope rate noise
spectral density 0.005 deg/s/sqrt(Hz) and accelerometer noise power
spectral density 400 ug/sqrt(Hz). The bias, scale, misalignment,
temperature-slope, hard- and soft-iron values in the samples are
illustrative choices, not datasheet values.

## 7. Synthetic data

`src/core/synth.js`, seeded with mulberry32 and Box-Muller normal samples:

- motion scripts of holds and smooth rotations about body axes
  (`(1 - cos)` rate profile, integrated exactly to the commanded angle);
- accelerometer `a_raw = D f + b + n` with `D = diag(s) (I + coupling)`,
  white noise of the given density, hand jitter while moving, optional
  random placement error per pose;
- gyroscope `w_raw = diag(s) w + b0 + k (T - 25) + n` with an exponential
  warm-up of the die temperature;
- magnetometer `m_raw = S m_body + c + n` during a seeded random tumble;
- stationary noise: white noise, first-order Gauss-Markov bias (correlation
  time 60 s) and rate random walk.
